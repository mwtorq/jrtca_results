JRTCA Jack Russell Terrier Trials

Trials provide an opportunity for Jack Russell Terrier owners to get to know each other, get together with terrier friends and in general have a good time with their terriers. They also provide the necessary opportunity to see breeding stock from around the country. This interaction between fellow breeders, as well as between breeders and other Jack Russell enthusiasts, fosters continued improvement in the breeding of quality terriers.

The JRTCA sanctions trials throughout the US to ensure a fair and standardized competition and the safety of exhibitors and terriers. Terriers and their owners compete in Conformation, Go-to-Ground, Racing, Agility, Obedience, Youth Division and Trailing and Locating.

Sanctioned Events

Conformation classes are judged much like any other dog show. The winner is the dog that most closely matches the breed standard. In addition to conformation and movement, the dog is judged on temperament; as in all things having to do with Jack Russells, the best working dog is being sought.

Racing is perhaps the most exciting of all the terrier trial events. A sanctioned track is a straight course with a starting box at one end and a stack of straw bales (the finish line) at the other. A lure is attached to a piece of string that is pulled along by a generator. The dogs are muzzled for safety; theirs and the catchers. The first dog through the hole in the straw bales is the winner.

Go-to-Ground is an event that simulates a hunting situation. The terrier traverses a tunnel (10 or 30 feet) and attempts to find a quarry at the end of the tunnel (a rat protected by a cage). The tunnel has a one or two turns to make it a little harder for the terrier to complete the course. The terrier with the fastest time to the quarry is considered the winner. The terrier must mark (i.e., bark, scratch, whine) for a predetermined period of time in order to qualify the time.

Agility involves both terrier and handler. The handler must guide the terrier (either on or off-lead) over a course (jumps, tunnels, see-saw, weave poles, etc). The terrier/handler with the fewest faults is the winner. This is a timed event in case of a tie.

Obedience involves both terrier and handler. Handler and terrier are required to perform a series of exercises that test the obedience of the terrier. Each exercise is graded by the obedience judge. Points may be subtracted for minor and substantial faults.

Youth competitors are expected to be on time, neat and workman-like, listen to and follow directions and show a genuine effort to communicate and work with their terrier. There is a great need to offer things for our youth to do with their terriers, particularly as it also provides a chance to learn.

Trailing and Locating involves the terrier tracking and locating a quarry (above ground). The terrier is judged on his ability to follow a line in a simulated natural hunting environment, to locate, mark and open on the quarry. The terrier is judged on a combination of time and accuracy. The terrier with the fastest time is not necessarily the winner.

Non-Sanctioned Events

High Jump, also known as Puissance, is a fun, non-sanctioned event offered at some terrier trials. Each terrier must jump over a set a bars without knocking them down. The bars are raised after each round. The terrier who jumps the highest, wins the event.
