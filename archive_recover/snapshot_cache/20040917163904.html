Changes to Trial Year 2000

As the popularity of the Jack Russell Terrier grows, so does the membership of the JRTCC.  With growth comes changes.  The rules for the JRTCC sanctioned trial were written over ten years ago, and were based on those of the JRTCGB and the JRTCA.  All changes were discussed at length by the Board, taking into consideration all letters, e-mails and comments that have been received in the past years.  As you will notice, the vast amount of changes have come in Agility.

For those of you who plan on attending JRTCC sanctioned trials this year I hope the changes suit your needs.  For those of you who will be putting on sanctioned trials this year, a new package will be sent that you should read carefully.

We are please to announce the following changes to this years trial package:

Conformation:

Bred-by-Exhibitor dogs and bitches will now be included in the Open Division, and be considered for Open Championship.

Spay/Neuter class will be mandatory this year only, in the Misc. Division.  It will be open to spay/neutered terriers one year and older.  It will be judged on breed standard and may be split by sex if there are enough entries.

Go-to-Ground:

There shall be Go-to-Ground Championships awarded to the first and second finisher in Under (10”-12.5”) and to the first and second finisher in Over (12.5”-15”).

Agility:

Dogs under 1 year of age MAY NOT participate in any class or practice run.  This is for their health and safety.

Suggested Agility Classes

Note:  Cross-entering only between Agility II and Agility III classes.

Agility I:  On Lead:  For beginner dogs held over a simple course.  Does not include weave poles or teeter totter.

Agility II:  Off Lead:  Does not include weave poles.

Agility III:  Off Lead:  Over a more challenging course including weave poles.

Agility Champion shall be awarded to best combined score from Agility II and Agility III.

A National or Regional Final Agility Champion shall be awarded to best combined score from Agility II, Agility III and Jumpers class.

JRTCC REQUIREMENTS FOR AGILITY EQUIPMENT:

Most people’s comments about Agility at JRTCC trials centered around the lack of standard equipment or simply put, the lack of equipment size and standards regulations.  But in the end, the overall consensus was that to put strict equipment regulations in place would all but eliminate Agility from our trials.  Agility equipment is difficult to rent and transport and at this point, the club doesn’t own any equipment, nor are there any plans to buy any.  A few people in the club have built equipment to various standards or non-standards, and make them available to the club so this is what we use.  The agility equipment should, whenever possible, be an acceptable standard of the AAC, USDAA, or other legitimate Agility organization.

JRTCC SUGGESTIONS TO PROMOTE AGILITY WITHIN THE CLUB

1. In order to promote more off leash competitors you could include the following to the Agility I course:  The last obstacle would be the pause table.  After the required time to complete the obstacle the clock would be stopped.  That would represent the final  time.  The handler would then remove the leash and attempt three jumps in a straight line off the pause table.  Each completed jump would be worth 5 bonus points added to the final score.

2. In order to promote better competition over all, the weave poles should be available to practice on the outside of the agility area throughout the day.

Conclusions:

All of the above changes are a result of comments, suggestions or complaints that have been sent to the Board.  Hopefully these changes will suit the vast majority of the members as the Board feels that the club must serve its members.  All these changes will be voted upon at the next AGM.
